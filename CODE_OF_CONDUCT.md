# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as contributors, maintainers, and community members pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free, respectful, and welcoming experience for everyone.

We welcome people of all backgrounds and identities, including but not limited to people of any age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, sexual identity, sexual orientation, neurotype, language, technical skill level, or employment status.

We are committed to building a project community where people can contribute, learn, ask questions, share feedback, and collaborate without fear of harassment, humiliation, exclusion, or abuse.

---

## Our Values

This project aims to encourage:

- Respectful collaboration
- Clear and constructive communication
- Curiosity, learning, and experimentation
- Good-faith discussion and disagreement
- Inclusive participation from beginners and experienced contributors alike
- Safety, privacy, and responsible behaviour
- A focus on improving the project and supporting the community

---

## Expected Behaviour

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive community include:

- Using welcoming and inclusive language
- Being respectful of differing viewpoints and experiences
- Giving and accepting constructive feedback gracefully
- Focusing on what is best for the project and community
- Showing empathy toward other community members
- Being patient with newcomers and people learning new tools
- Asking for clarification instead of assuming bad intent
- Respecting boundaries when someone asks to disengage
- Giving credit where appropriate
- Keeping discussions relevant, useful, and professional
- Reporting security issues responsibly through the appropriate channel
- Helping maintain a safe, respectful, and productive environment

---

## Unacceptable Behaviour

Examples of unacceptable behaviour include, but are not limited to:

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking, or sustained disruption
- Sexualized language, imagery, jokes, or unwanted sexual attention
- Trolling, baiting, insulting, mocking, or deliberately inflammatory comments
- Personal attacks, name-calling, or derogatory remarks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing or threatening to publish someone else’s private information, such as email addresses, physical addresses, phone numbers, private messages, workplace details, identity documents, IP addresses, or location data, without explicit permission
- Discriminatory jokes, slurs, stereotypes, or exclusionary behaviour
- Threats of violence or encouragement of self-harm
- Repeatedly contacting someone after they have asked you to stop
- Deliberately derailing discussions, issues, pull requests, or project spaces
- Spamming, flooding, or abusing project communication channels
- Impersonating another person, maintainer, contributor, or organization
- Submitting malicious code, intentionally harmful content, or deceptive contributions
- Abusing project resources, automation, bots, issue trackers, discussions, or CI systems
- Retaliating against someone for reporting a concern or participating in an investigation
- Any other behaviour that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional, collaborative, or open-source setting

---

## Project Spaces Covered

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces, including but not limited to:

- GitHub repositories
- Issues, pull requests, discussions, commits, comments, and wikis
- Project documentation
- Chat rooms, forums, or community servers
- Social media accounts connected to the project
- Email communication related to the project
- Online or offline events where someone is representing the project
- Any place where a person is acting as an official representative of the project or community

This Code of Conduct also applies when behaviour outside project spaces directly affects the safety, reputation, participation, or well-being of the project community.

---

## Maintainer Responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for:

- Clarifying standards of acceptable behaviour
- Applying this Code of Conduct fairly and consistently
- Taking reasonable action when unacceptable behaviour is reported or observed
- Protecting reporters from retaliation where possible
- Respecting confidentiality during investigations
- Removing, editing, or rejecting contributions that violate this Code of Conduct
- Temporarily or permanently restricting participation when necessary
- Avoiding conflicts of interest when handling reports
- Documenting enforcement decisions where appropriate

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, reject, or block comments, commits, code, issues, pull requests, wiki edits, discussions, or other contributions that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct.

Maintainers may also temporarily or permanently ban contributors for behaviour they consider inappropriate, threatening, offensive, harmful, abusive, or disruptive.

---

## Reporting a Problem

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behaviour may be reported by contacting the project team at:

**kai9987kai@gmail.com**

When reporting an incident, please include as much detail as you feel comfortable providing, such as:

- What happened
- Where it happened
- When it happened
- Who was involved
- Any relevant links, screenshots, messages, commits, issues, or pull requests
- Whether the behaviour is ongoing
- Whether you feel there is an immediate safety concern
- What outcome you are hoping for, if any

You do not need to provide every detail to make a report. Reports can still be reviewed even if some information is missing.

---

## Confidentiality

The project team will make reasonable efforts to protect the privacy and confidentiality of anyone who reports an incident.

Information about a report will only be shared with people who need it in order to review, investigate, or resolve the issue.

The identity of a reporter will not be intentionally disclosed without permission unless required for safety, legal reasons, or meaningful enforcement.

---

## Anti-Retaliation Policy

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern, participates in an investigation, supports a reporter, or helps enforce this Code of Conduct is not allowed.

Retaliation may include harassment, intimidation, exclusion, threats, public shaming, bad-faith complaints, or attempts to damage someone’s reputation because they raised a concern.

Retaliation is itself a violation of this Code of Conduct and may result in enforcement action.

---

## Enforcement Process

When a report is received, maintainers will review the available information and decide what action is appropriate.

Possible steps may include:

1. Acknowledging the report
2. Reviewing evidence and context
3. Contacting involved parties where appropriate
4. Asking for additional information if needed
5. Deciding whether the Code of Conduct was violated
6. Taking corrective or enforcement action
7. Communicating the outcome where appropriate

The project team will aim to respond in a timely and fair way. Some cases may require more time depending on complexity, evidence, availability, and safety concerns.

---

## Enforcement Actions

Depending on the severity and context of the behaviour, maintainers may take one or more of the following actions:

### 1. Correction

A private or public clarification explaining why the behaviour was inappropriate, with a request to correct it.

### 2. Warning

A formal warning that continued behaviour may lead to stronger action.

### 3. Content Removal

Removal or editing of comments, issues, pull requests, commits, documentation, discussions, or other content that violates this Code of Conduct.

### 4. Temporary Restrictions

Temporary limits on participation, such as being blocked from commenting, opening issues, submitting pull requests, or joining discussions.

### 5. Temporary Ban

Temporary removal from project spaces for a defined period.

### 6. Permanent Ban

Permanent removal from project spaces.

### 7. Escalation

If there is a credible threat to safety, illegal activity, or severe abuse, maintainers may escalate the issue to platform administrators, hosting providers, or appropriate authorities.

---

## Appeals

A person who receives enforcement action may appeal by contacting:

**kai9987kai@gmail.com**

Appeals should include:

- The enforcement action being appealed
- Why the person believes the decision should be reconsidered
- Any relevant context or evidence
- A commitment to follow the Code of Conduct going forward, where appropriate

Appeals will be reviewed by maintainers who were not directly involved in the original decision where possible.

---

## Conflicts of Interest

If a report involves a maintainer, close collaborator, friend, employer, employee, or anyone else where there may be a conflict of interest, that person should not be the sole decision-maker for the report.

Where possible, another maintainer or trusted project representative should review the situation.

---

## Good-Faith Participation

This Code of Conduct is intended to protect the community, not to punish honest mistakes.

People may make mistakes, misunderstand tone, or use unclear wording. In many cases, a respectful correction or clarification may be enough.

However, repeated harmful behaviour, refusal to respect boundaries, or bad-faith conduct may lead to stronger enforcement action.

Knowingly false, malicious, or bad-faith reports may also be treated as a violation of this Code of Conduct.

---

## Security and Responsible Disclosure

If your concern involves a security vulnerability, exploit, leaked secret, unsafe code path, or sensitive technical issue, please avoid posting details publicly.

Report security-related concerns privately to:

**kai9987kai@gmail.com**

This helps protect users, contributors, and the project while the issue is reviewed.

---

## Community Health

Maintainers and contributors are encouraged to help keep the project healthy by:

- Welcoming new contributors
- Keeping discussions focused and constructive
- Avoiding unnecessary hostility or gatekeeping
- Helping people understand contribution guidelines
- Reporting harmful behaviour early
- Supporting respectful disagreement
- Encouraging safe experimentation and responsible development

A strong project is not just built from code. It is also built from trust, respect, and shared responsibility.
